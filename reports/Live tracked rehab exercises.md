# Add wall push-ups, chair stands and Otago strength next

RecoverLens should add the **wall push-up family (wall, incline, knee, floor)** first, then the **30-second chair stand**, then the chair-supported **Otago strength set**: seated knee extension, standing hamstring curl, heel raise and standing hip abduction. After those come the glute bridge, wall slide, scaption, plank, split squat, step-up, marching, tandem stance and neck side bend. That makes 15 exercises, and each needs only a wall, a chair or a step. Push-ups are the only exercise with widely repeated open-source thresholds: **elbow above 160° at the top, below 90° at the bottom, and a shoulder-hip-ankle body line of 170–180°**. Every other threshold in this report is a published clinical protocol value, a value from one open-source rehab code base (physiocore), or a proposed starting value that needs a `// TUNE:` marker. Each one is labelled. Most of the shortlist fits the current `ExerciseConfig` with small engine additions: a `hip` joint, a normalised heel-rise metric, a leg-to-trunk abduction metric, a body-line hold gate and a timed-set mode. Camera tracking alone no longer sets a product apart, because Physitrack is shipping a 33-point webcam tracker of the same model class. A RecoverLens entry for Challenge 09 should therefore pair a small set of accurately tracked exercises with objective function tests, an honest accuracy check, measured low data use and task-shifting tools for a country with **about 0.26 physiotherapists per 10,000 people**.

## Push-ups are the only exercise with consensus thresholds

Most open-source live trackers are gym counters, and they agree on very little apart from push-ups. MediaPipe push-up counters almost all drive the count from the shoulder-elbow-wrist angle (landmarks 11-13-15 or 12-14-16). They use **up above 160° and down below 90°**, and one variant uses 150°/70° with a 1 s debounce ([HassanRasheed91/Pushup-Detection](https://github.com/HassanRasheed91/Pushup-Detection); [Labellerr](https://www.labellerr.com/blog/ai-powered-pushup-counter-form-corrector/); [HF khelverse push_up.py](https://huggingface.co/spaces/khelverse/live-train/blob/main/exercises/push_up.py)). Body alignment is usually the **shoulder-hip-ankle angle, with 170–180° counted as straight and anything under 170° flagged as hip sag** ([Labellerr](https://www.labellerr.com/blog/ai-powered-pushup-counter-form-corrector/)). Conventions still vary: one demo uses hip-knee-ankle instead ([niharpalem](https://niharpalem.substack.com/p/push-up-form-analysis-using-mediapipe)). The broader repo consensus is knee ≤90–95° at squat or lunge depth, elbow ≤90° at the bottom of a push-up, hip 160–180° for a straight body line, curl contraction below 45° and extension above 150°, and overhead lockout above 165° ([Pranitha-27, no licence](https://github.com/Pranitha-27/AI-Based-Gym-Exercise-Posture-Corrector-and-Repetition-Counter)). All of these values come from gym use, so rehab users need gentler targets.

The only rehab-specific code base the researchers could read in full is **physiocore / PhysioPlus**, and it supplies the only sourced thresholds for lying exercises ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/physiocore/0.2.6/); [GitHub](https://github.com/pankajmaurya/PhysioPlus)). Its glute bridge rests at a shoulder-hip-knee angle of 100–130° and counts the raised position at **155–180°**, with the knees bent 10–90°, a lying-posture check and a 2–5 s hold. Its straight leg raise keeps the knee at 150–180°, rests at a shoulder-hip-ankle angle of 160–180°, counts the raised position at 90–158° and needs a 3 s hold. Its ankle pumps move between 80–110° and 130–180°. physiocore needs the hold before it counts a rep. RecoverLens should not copy that rule. Under CLAUDE.md rule 4, a rep counts as soon as the patient reaches the target band, and the hold, depth and fault rules only decide whether it also counts toward `correctReps`.

Clinical protocols supply the remaining sourced values. The CDC STEADI 30-second chair stand uses an armless chair with the arms crossed on the chest, counts as many full stands as possible in 30 s, and **counts a stand that is more than halfway up at the 30 s mark** ([NJ/CDC STEADI](https://www.nj.gov/humanservices/doas/forms/STEADI-Assessment-30Sec-508.pdf)). In the protocol, needing the arms to stand scores zero. RecoverLens should record arm use as an observation rather than stop the test (rule 2). The MGH ACL protocol makes "SLR without extension lag" a progression criterion ([Mass General](https://www.massgeneral.org/assets/MGH/pdf/orthopaedics/sports-medicine/physical-therapy/rehabilitation-protocol-for-ACL.pdf)), and a hip protocol cues raising the leg "8–10 inches" ([American Hip Institute](https://www.benjamindombmd.com/pdf/gluteus-medius-repair-pt-protocol.pdf)).

Camera placement matters more than the choice of model. A 2026 JMIR mHealth study tested 12 phone set-ups with 22 participants. Diagonal and frontal views at 180–200 cm worked best: squats reached **95.5% detection from a 200 cm diagonal, against 0% from a 90 cm side view** where the body was cut off ([Oliosi et al., JMIR mHealth 2026](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2026/1/e82412/)). The failure came from poor framing, not from the side view itself. Side-view exercises should therefore require ankles and shoulders to be visible, not avoid the side view. Under good conditions, MediaPipe knee flexion shows a mean bias of about **+1.5° against goniometer-set targets** ([PubMed 42976294](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42976294)), but accuracy "quickly decreases" when conditions are less favourable ([Dill et al. 2023](https://doaj.org/article/b1b1d4db2666494cacf7bcbcee8cdaa3)). A Chalmers thesis found trunk orientation and normalised displacement more stable than 2D knee flexion ([Chalmers](https://odr.chalmers.se/bitstreams/6e3607e0-8e8c-4f7f-b33e-c92bfab18210/download)). This supports displacement metrics for heel raises and step-ups. BlazePose's z coordinate is not metric ([ML Kit model card](https://developers.google.cn/static/ml-kit/images/vision/pose-detection/pose_model_card.pdf)), so it should only decide left versus right or the direction of a turn, never drive a threshold. The existing front-view squat's `depth: true` with `relativeRange` is the one tested exception.

"Engine work" names what the current types do not yet support: `Joint` today is only `shoulder | elbow | knee`, and `PrimarySpec` adds only `hipRise`.

The first four lead because they need almost no engine work and rest on the strongest combination of evidence. Push-ups have the only mature open-source tracking, and the wall version suits frail and post-op users. The 30-second chair stand is a CDC STEADI falls test with age-band cut-offs, for example **below 14 stands for men and below 12 for women aged 60–64** ([NJ/CDC STEADI](https://www.nj.gov/humanservices/doas/forms/STEADI-Assessment-30Sec-508.pdf); [bpac NZ](https://bpac.org.nz/falls/docs/The_30-Second_Chair_Stand_Test.pdf)). A markerless smartphone app already matched a reference system on the 30-second sit-to-stand and TUG with **ICC 0.812–0.995** ([JMIR Aging 2025](https://aging.jmir.org/2025/1/e67322)). It is the existing `sit_to_stand` config plus a 30 s clock. Seated knee extension and the hamstring curl are the Otago knee extensor and flexor exercises, and Otago's chair-supported strength set (knee extensor, knee flexor, hip abductor, calf and toe raises) has reduced falls and fall injuries ([UNC Otago](https://www.med.unc.edu/aging/cgwep/courses/otago-exercise-program/otago-videos/strength-exercises/); [OUH Otago booklet](https://www.ouh.nhs.uk/media/5xydterh/85619otago.pdf)). One warning applies to seated knee extension: a Sensors review lists it among the movements MediaPipe finds harder because of unusual pose and occlusion ([Sensors 24(19):6325](https://mdpi.com/1424-8220/24/19/6325)), and a monocular BlazePose study measured its worst error, **62.8 mm, on seated knee extension** ([IJACSA 17(10)](https://thesai.org/Downloads/Volume17No10/Paper_65-Accuracy_and_Reliability_of_Monocular_3D_Pose.pdf)). A strict side view with the chair side-on, plus `relativeRange`, should reduce the error.

Close runners-up are configuration variants with little engine work: overhead triceps extension (elbow 70 → 150 [P], a mirror of the curl), overhead press (elbow 80 → 160 with the wrist above the nose [P]), standing shoulder extension, standing hip extension (side view, thigh 10–20° behind vertical [P]), seated or band row, bird-dog, and stroke reach-to-target or hand-to-mouth. MediaPipe tracking of post-stroke reaching was shown feasible, but only with N = 7 ([Unger et al.](https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/en/publications/using-mediapipe-to-track-upper-limb-reaching-movements-after-stro/)), and it detected compensation less well than optical motion capture ([Frontiers Med 2025](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2025.1645369/pdf)).

The fault wording needs care. Frontal-plane knee valgus agreed well with 3D motion capture in one study, with an excursion error of 2.4° ([JSSM 2024](https://www.jssm.org/jssm-23-515.xml-Fulltext)). A 2026 review, however, concludes that 2D video does not support lower-limb frontal-plane kinematics where high accuracy is needed ([Janisch et al. 2026](https://www.medicalschool-hamburg.de/files/inhalte/News/2026/2026-janisch_et_al-bmc_musculoskeletal_disorders.pdf)). Valgus, pelvic drop, knee-past-toe and shrug flags should therefore trigger only after the fault has lasted beyond the existing 300 ms debounce, and should read as observations ("knees drifting inward — try pushing them over your toes"), never as risks or injuries (rule 9). A rep with a flagged fault still counts and is only left out of `correctReps`.

## Single-camera blind spots belong in guided timer mode

Some exercises move along the camera's line of sight, have no landmark to track, or use a pose the model was not trained on. These should run as `GuidedExerciseConfig` entries: steps, voice count and a timer, with no score. **Shoulder external and internal rotation with the elbow at the side** (already guided) rotate along the camera axis. A Kobe University study needed machine learning on top of MediaPipe to estimate rotation from frontal video ([Kobe Univ.](https://da.lib.kobe-u.ac.jp/da/kernel/0100489645/0100489645.pdf)). The 90/90 "cactus" variant seen from the front is the only rotation that could move to live tracking later. **Pelvic tilt** and **cat-camel** have no lumbar or spine landmarks. **Dead bug**, and supine work in general, is out of distribution for adult-trained pose models ([arXiv 2406.17382](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17382v2)). **Neck rotation** and **seated trunk rotation** happen out of the image plane, so they can only be reported as a percentage of the person's baseline turn, never in degrees. **Quad sets** produce no visible motion. **Terminal knee extension** and **toe raises** move only 5–10°, close to landmark jitter. **Clamshell** and **side-lying hip abduction** are partly transverse-plane and suffer bottom-leg occlusion. **Heel-to-toe walking** and **TUG** take the person out of frame, although TUG total time is possible with the phone 4–5 m away ([IEEE MetroXRAINE TUG](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/f7bce5f8-8a8c-4b3a-aef1-67d28306ef4e/IEEEMetroXraine_Pose_detection_TUG.pdf)), and the **pendulum swing** is already guided. **Straight leg raise** and **heel slide** sit on the border. Both are guided today, and physiocore's SLR bands (rest 160–180°, raised 90–158°, knee ≥150°, 3 s hold) make an experimental live version possible behind the lying-posture gate. No MediaPipe validation in degrees exists for any supine or prone exercise, so they should stay guided until fixtures from real patients show they work.

The most practical test plan is to run REHAB24-6 RGB video through MediaPipe to produce `replay.test.ts` fixtures, then use UI-PRMD's joint-angle trajectories to check ranges of motion and how well correct and incorrect versions separate. A cheap upgrade path is physio-recorded templates: record the "up" and "down" key poses once and count transitions by nearest key pose with hysteresis. That is PoseRAC's idea without the machine learning, and it effectively generates an `ExerciseConfig` automatically. Matching a 1–3 s template against a single angle channel costs around 10⁴ operations per update, which is trivial in TypeScript.

## Incumbents win on clinicians and hardware, which Zimbabwe lacks

The market splits into four groups. **Sword** (Phoenix voice AI plus the Thrive Pad and US physical therapists) and **Hinge** (TrueMotion "Movement Analysis" of joint angles, symmetry and endurance, plus the Enso wearable) are enterprise virtual clinics paid for by US employers ([Sword](https://swordhealth.com/articles/introducing-phoenix); [Hinge PR](https://s205.q4cdn.com/978984498/files/doc_news/Hinge-Health-unveils-new-AI-powered-care-tools-2025.pdf)). **Kemtai** (2,000+ camera exercises, validated against Vicon at ALYN Hospital, 72% adherence "data on file") and **Exer** (about 144 iOS assessments, Class II 510(k)-exempt, Epic integration at Mayo) sell camera-only tools to providers ([Kemtai](https://kemtai.com/faqs/); [Exer](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/exer-scan/id6466210809)). **Physitrack/PhysiApp** is adding a 33-point webcam tracker for its 500 most-prescribed exercises, with no custom exercises, and automates US remote monitoring billing at $8 per patient per month ([Physitrack](https://www.physitrack.com/motion-capture); [RTM](https://www.physitrack.com/remote-therapeutic-monitoring-rtm)). **Kaia** has the strongest trial: in the Rise-uP RCT of about 1,240 patients, **pain fell 46% versus 24% at 12 months**, though the package also included GP support ([pharmaphorum](https://pharmaphorum.com/news/kaia-app-for-lower-back-pain-shows-its-worth-in-large-scale-trial)). Its Motion Coach was non-inferior to physiotherapist exercise evaluations in hip and knee OA ([Kaia](https://kaiahealth.com/newsroom/press-releases/clinical-study-kaia-health-computer-vision-technology-as-accurate-as-physical-therapists-in-suggesting-exercise-corrections/)). Most incumbent evidence is self-sponsored. Hinge's S-1 says all its cited studies were company-supported ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1673743/000119312525051004/d829170ds1.htm)), and Milliman did not assess whether Thrive causes Sword's reported savings ([Milliman](https://media.milliman.com/v1/media/edge/images/millimaninc5660-milliman6442-prod27d5-0001/media/Milliman/PDFs/2025-Articles/12-17-25_Independent-Review-Report-SWD-Methodology.pdf)). selfBACK's trial shows the risk of low uptake: the effect was small and larger among adherent users ([Rughani 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ejp.2080)).

The local context is the opportunity. Zimbabwe has **0.26 practising physiotherapists per 10,000 people** ([World Physiotherapy 2023](https://world.physio/sites/default/files/2024-01/AMC2023-Zimbabwe.pdf)), roughly one per 38,000. Many residents spend **over 10% of monthly income on internet access** against the UN's 2% target ([MISA Zimbabwe](https://zimbabwe.misa.org/2026/08/16/internet-affordability-and-access-in-zimbabwe/)). In sub-Saharan Africa, telerehab for low back pain matched conventional physio with high adherence in three Nigerian RCTs, but limited smartphone access and digital literacy held it back ([BMC Digital Health 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s44247-026-00261-0)). LMIC barriers include unstable internet and power ([PMC12726847](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12726847/)). The non-exercise features that would make the entry stand out copy what the incumbents charge for, rebuilt for these constraints. First, **camera function tests with norms**: the 30-second chair stand, single-leg stance time, TUG total time and active ROM, collected into a shareable "ROM and function report" exported as FHIR Observations, matching Hinge Movement Analysis and Exer at no hardware cost. Second, **an honest accuracy mini-study** comparing RecoverLens angles with a goniometer on 10–20 volunteers and reporting mean absolute error, which answers Kemtai and Kaia's validation claims. Third, **a measured low-data mode**: kilobytes per session, a one-time model download, summaries only with no video, queued sync, and an SMS fallback for nudges. Fourth, **task-shifting**: a community health worker or caregiver mode with several patients on one device and red/amber/green triage for the physio. Fifth, **a frame-rate target on low-end Android**, in contrast to Exer's iOS-only app and Sword's hardware. Sixth, **audio-first, low-literacy screens in chiShona and isiNdebele**. Finally, **a pre-registered pilot plan** with a Zimbabwean physio department, measuring adherence, the 30-second chair stand, pain on a 0–10 scale and the Patient-Specific Functional Scale. WhatsApp's dominance in the region and judges' scoring criteria are reasonable assumptions, but the research did not verify them.

## Conclusion

This shortlist mostly needs geometry, not new models. Four additions cover 13 of the 15 exercises: a `hip` joint, a normalised displacement metric, a segment-to-trunk angle, and body-line and foot-alignment hold gates. A 30 s timed set turns the existing sit-to-stand into a validated falls test. With those additions, RecoverLens can turn exercises into outcome measures, so the same camera session that counts reps also produces a number a physiotherapist can trust and track over time. That shifts the pitch from "an AI rep counter", which Physitrack now commoditises, to "objective rehab measurement for one physiotherapist per 38,000 people".

The weakest link is threshold validity, not code. Apart from push-up angles, physiocore's lying bands and CDC protocol rules, every number in the table is a proposal. Rehab ranges for frail, post-op and older users are not in any open data set. The fastest way to credibility is to record `?debug=1` fixtures from real patients and a local physiotherapist for the top five exercises, tune the `[P]` values against them, and publish the error numbers.
